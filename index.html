Waves & Light Encyclopedia

A. Vibrations & Waves

Vibration is a back-and-forth motion through an equilibrium point (e.g., guitar strings, pendulums).

Key Parameters:
• 1 Vibration = A → B → C → B → A
• Period (T): Time for 1 vibration (T = t/n) [s]
• Frequency (f): Vibrations per second (f = n/t) [Hz]
• Formula: f = 1/T atau T = 1/f

Types of Waves:

  • Mechanical Waves: Need a medium (e.g., Sound, Water waves).
  • Electromagnetic Waves: Can travel in a vacuum (e.g., Light, X-rays).
  • Transverse: Vibration ⊥ Propagation (Up-down motion).
  • Longitudinal: Vibration || Propagation (Compressions & Rarefactions).
Wave Speed Formula: v = λ × f

B. Sound Waves

Sound is a longitudinal mechanical wave. It cannot travel in space (vacuum)!

Frequency Ranges:
• Infrasonic: < 20 Hz
• Audiosonic: 20 - 20,000 Hz
• Ultrasonic: > 20,000 Hz
Characteristics:
• Pitch depends on Frequency.
• Loudness depends on Amplitude.

C. Light Properties

Light is an electromagnetic wave ($v = 3 \times 10^8$ m/s).

  • Straight Line: Light travels straight unless hit an object.
  • Reflection: Angle of incidence (i) = Angle of reflection (r).
  • Refraction (Bending): Happens when light moves between different optical densities (e.g., Air to Water).

D. Human Eye & Optical Instruments

The Path of Sight:

Cornea → Pupil → Aqueous Humor → Lens → Vitreous Humor → Retina → Optic Nerve → Brain.

Vision Defects:

Myopia (Nearsighted):
Focused in front of retina. Blurry far away. Use Concave (-) lens.
Hyperopia (Farsighted):
Focused behind retina. Blurry up close. Use Convex (+) lens.

Lens Power (Diopter):

The strength of a lens is measured in Diopters (D).

D = 1 / f
*(Note: focal length (f) must be in meters)*

3. Steps to Follow

  1. Hold the lens (eyeglasses) between a light source and a screen (white paper).
  2. Slowly move the lens back and forth until a sharp, focused spot of light appears on the screen.
  3. Measure the distance (in meters) from the lens to the screen (this is focal length / f).
  4. Calculate the Diopter using formula: D = 1 / f.
  5. For concave lenses (myopia), use a negative value (-).
