Understanding Waves

A wave is a vibration that propagates through a medium or space. Waves transfer energy, not matter.

f = 1 / T (Frequency)
v = λ × f (Wave Speed)
  • Transverse Waves: Vibration is perpendicular to the direction of propagation (e.g., Light waves).
  • Longitudinal Waves: Vibration is parallel to the direction of propagation (e.g., Sound waves).

Properties of Light

Light is an electromagnetic wave that can travel through a vacuum at 300,000 km/s.

1. Reflection

Light bouncing off a surface. Law: Angle of Incidence = Angle of Reflection.

2. Refraction

The bending of light as it passes from one medium to another (e.g., air to water). This is how lenses work!

The Human Eye & Vision

The eye focuses light on the Retina to form an image. When the eye can't focus perfectly, we use lenses.

  • Myopia (Nearsightedness): Light focuses in front of the retina. Corrected by Concave (-) lenses.
  • Hyperopia (Farsightedness): Light focuses behind the retina. Corrected by Convex (+) lenses.
Lens Power (D) = 1 / f (in meters)

Waves, Light & The Global Goals

How does physics help the world? Let's look at the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs):

SDG 3: Good Health and Well-being

Wave technology is the backbone of modern medicine. X-rays help detect bone fractures, and Ultrasounds (sound waves) allow doctors to see babies inside the womb or check internal organs without surgery.

SDG 7: Affordable and Clean Energy

The properties of light allow us to use Solar Panels (Photovoltaic cells). These cells capture electromagnetic radiation from sunlight and convert it directly into clean, renewable electricity.

SDG 9: Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure

Radio waves and Microwaves enable Global Communication. Wi-Fi, 5G, and Satellite signals use waves to keep the world connected, fostering innovation in digital infrastructure.

Module Summary

Waves & Light

Waves carry energy. Light is a special wave that travels in straight lines but can bend (refract) when hitting a lens.

Vision Correction

Lenses change how light enters the eye. Convex lenses (+) for far-sighted, Concave lenses (-) for near-sighted.

Key Concept: Science isn't just in books; it's in your glasses, your phone's Wi-Fi, and the hospital's X-ray machine!
